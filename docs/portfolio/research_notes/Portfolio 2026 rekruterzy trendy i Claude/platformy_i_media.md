## 1. Behance: which 2025-2026 projects are featured or most appreciated, how are they structured, which devices recur, how long are they?

### Takeaway
Behance's strongest branding, packaging, advertising and motion projects are text-light vertical stacks of about 10-33 image/GIF/video modules (median about 20, about 19,000 px tall at 1440 px width) with one short concept paragraph (median about 440 characters of live text in total) and an optional role-by-role credits block; none of the 17 projects I measured shows results or KPIs as text. "Featured" (curator ribbon, hand-picked, reviewed once at first publication) is a different list from "most appreciated" (monthly top lists dominated by portfolio decks and food/coffee/lifestyle identities with 2.8-12.5K appreciations), and AI-tagged work does get featured when a human art-direction story is visible.

### Cited Findings

#### How Behance defines "featured" (official guidance)
- Best of Behance is described on the gallery page as "Featuring standout projects hand-picked by our curation team"; the category bar offers Best of Behance plus Graphic Design, Photography, Illustration, 3D Art, UI/UX, Motion, etc. Cards on 2026-10-29 carried only ~50-490 appreciations (e.g. Toormix "Dansàneu 2026" about 49 appreciations and 357 views), so featuring is curator-driven and fast, not popularity-driven — [Best of Behance gallery](https://www.behance.net/galleries/best-of-behance) (screenshot `behance_best_of_behance_gallery.jpg`)
- Behance's own guide (published 14 Aug 2025): 19 million monthly visitors, 100+ curated galleries, every featured project hand-selected; curators look for work that is "visually striking, thoughtfully conceived, and executed with skill and precision" and also weigh "how it's presented, the context behind it, and the organic traction"; they review a project only once, at first publication, so publish only complete projects; recommended 5-15 images, projects with fewer than 3 images are not seen; images 1400 px wide; "mockups are a valuable resource"; a few sentences on scope, client, process and inspiration "goes a long way"; the cover should show the look and feel, include a full-size version of the cover inside the project, and needs no text on it; choose the creative field that matches your own role; tags quality over quantity; add tools to be eligible for Adobe tool galleries; add credits/collaborators; "We do not feature shops, publishers, or agencies if the artist is not included as a collaborator" — [Behance Blog, How to Get Featured on Behance](https://www.behance.net/blog/how-to-get-featured-on-behance)
- Featured projects carry ribbons that name the galleries. ### Inferences
- A senior-level case study in the Behance idiom is: hero cover (full-bleed wordmark/mascot/product, optionally an award badge) -> one short concept paragraph with a cultural or brand hook -> 3-5 slides of exploration/system -> mockups in context -> 2-4 moving loops -> credits by role. Narrative lives inside typographic slides rather than text modules (Disney and the AI Lingerie piece have zero live text), which is bad for search and for an LLM that must read the page; on an own site, keep the slides but also keep a real text layer.
- Results/KPIs are absent on Behance but present in Polish award write-ups (e.g. KTR's "Efekt Black", +10% sales, see section 5); adding a one-line result per case is a cheap differentiator for an agency-side senior AD.
- For Kamil's stack (Illustrator, After Effects, Photoshop, Blender 3D, which matches the Kokkōpi tool list) the Behance route is a distribution channel, not the canonical home: tools tags make a project eligible for Photoshop/After Effects galleries, but Behance will not feature agency work unless the individual artist is a collaborator, so project credits must name him personally.
- AI-assisted work is featurable (RANO, SPORTS DAILY) but the highest-appreciated AI-tagged items (100-620) sit far below human-crafted identities (1K-12K); spec campaigns for famous brands made with AI (e.l.f., Marc Jacobs, Byredo) attract 100-300 appreciations.

### Gaps
- No official "Best of 2025/2026" ranking exists; I used the rolling monthly sort and the default Featured sort of each gallery, so numbers are a 2026-10-29 snapshot and older projects (Jorixent, Petbarn, Animation Portfolio 2024) leak into "this month".
- Video/motion embeds do not render in headless Chromium (codec/security), so reel structure, loop length and breakdown videos on Behance could not be captured; "results" could be hidden inside image slides (no OCR done).
- Behance help pages are partly blocked by Cloudflare; curators' internal criteria beyond the official blog are unpublished; nothing found on Adobe Portfolio changes in 2025-2026.
- Which images in AI-tagged projects are AI-made is only known where the author says so (AI Lingerie, SPORTS DAILY).

## 2. Dribbble: trending portfolio-website designs, case-study shots, motion shots and Dribbble's own content in 2026

### Inferences
- Dribbble rewards UI/product motion and logo marks; print, packaging and campaign key visuals rarely surface, so for a campaign/packaging AD it is a teaser channel (a loop or a hero crop linking to the full case), not a primary home.
- Any shot containing AI should carry a one-line AI note in the description (platform recommendation) and must not be 100% generated.
- The 2026 "motion shot" look is a short loop of a device or hero object on a dark or saturated flat background with typographic hero text; for a Blender/After Effects specialist the equivalent is a 3-6 s seamless loop of a packaging render.

## 3. Pinterest: Pinterest Predicts 2026, other visual trends, and how creatives use it for exposure

### Takeaway
Pinterest Predicts 2026 lists 21 trends that, read for visual direction, point to maximalism, tactility, nostalgia, theatricality and deliberate asymmetry (Glamoratti, Neo Deco, Poetcore, Glitchy Glam, Pen Pals, Laced Up, FunHaus) and a 2026 palette of Cool Blue, Jade, Plum Noir, Wasabi and Persimmon; Pinterest also labels AI-modified images and offers a "see fewer" control. Evidence on how designers get exposure there is thin, generic and vendor-written.

## 7. How designers credibly show AI-assisted work, and is there backlash against AI-heavy portfolios?

### Takeaway
The credible pattern in 2026 is specific and early disclosure (what AI did, what the human did, which tools/models), human art direction made visible through process proof and consistency studies, and avoiding prompt-only output; platform and award rules (Rookies, Dribbble, Cannes, EU/Poland labelling) now formalise this. Backlash is documented against AI-looking ads, flyers and unlabelled content, while evidence for recruiters rejecting disclosed AI in portfolios was not found.

### Cited Findings

#### Rules that define "credible" disclosure
- The Rookies (awards/portfolio platform): disclosure required for any meaningful AI use (concept generation, reference images, assets, animation/code help, layout influenced by AI); "If you are unsure whether disclosure is needed, disclose"; put it under a heading such as "AI Disclosure" or "Tools & Process"; good: "AI tools were used during early concept exploration to generate visual references. All final assets were created manually." / "AI was used to generate a base texture, which was then fully repainted and modified by hand"; poor: "AI-assisted", "Used AI a bit"; not allowed even if disclosed: direct uploads of AI output as final, prompt-only or minimally edited outputs, entries where AI performed most of the execution; may request process work or breakdowns — [The Rookies](https://help.therookies.co/rookie-awards/how-to-disclose-ai-use-in-your-entries/)
- Dribbble: no wholly AI-generated shots; acknowledge AI in the description — [Dribbble Shot Guidelines](https://help.dribbble.com/en/articles/11056039-dribbble-shot-guidelines); Behance: allowed, tag tools, Content Credentials beta — [Behance Help](https://help.behance.net/hc/en-us/articles/13850092907931-Behance-and-Adobe-Firefly-FAQ); Pinterest: "AI modified" label and "see fewer" control — [Pinterest Newsroom](https://newsroom.pinterest.com/en-au/news/introducing-gen-ai-labels/)
- Cannes Lions tightened rules after the 2025 DM9 scandal (a Grand Prix withdrawn because of an AI-faked case film): mandatory AI disclosure, an AI-ethics review panel, agency and client sign-off, possible multi-year bans; D&AD already required disclosure of how AI was used — [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/advertising/cannes-lions-withdraws-grand-prix-after-discovery-of-ai-faked-case-film-introduces-stricter-rules-for-ai-disclosure-72359.htm) [via search summary], [Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/creativity/ad-awards-rush-to-tighten-ai-rules-amid-cannes-fallout/) [via search summary]
- EU: the Code of Practice on marking and labelling AI-generated content was finalised in June 2026, ahead of Article 50 duties applying on 2 Aug 2026 — [Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/de/insights/2026/06/european-commission-publishes-final-code-of-practice-on-marking-and-labelling-aigenerated-content) [via search summary]; Polish practice and wording are in section 5.
- Adobe's Content Authenticity app (public beta Apr 2025) lets creators attach Content Credentials with verified identity and links; Behance shows credentials in its image viewer (beta) — [Adobe Blog](https://blog.adobe.com/en/publish/2025/04/24/adobe-content-authenticity-now-public-beta-helps-creators-secure-attribution) [via search summary]

### Inferences
- A defensible AI block per case (my synthesis of Rookies, Polish labelling guidance and the Behance/Motionographer examples): (1) a heading "AI Disclosure" / "Narzędzia i proces" at the top of the case; (2) three short lines: what AI did, what I did, what stayed fully human; (3) tools and model versions named; (4) evidence of control: before/after, iteration sheet, a consistency study across variants, one process time-lapse; (5) for photoreal AI imagery add a crop-resistant label "wygenerowane przez AI" or "zmodyfikowane przy użyciu AI"; (6) never publish AI-reconstructed case footage as if it were real (the DM9 lesson).
- Balance matters: pairing AI-assisted cases with at least one visibly human-crafted case (analogue/3D/print process shown) matches the market's preference and Polish juries' rewards, while the "AI specialist" positioning is supported by showing systems and decisions rather than volume (Cape Morris, Unfold, Dribbble essays).
- A portfolio that itself uses a Claude-powered feature should disclose it in the same plain way (what the assistant does, which sources it reads), consistent with the labelling norms above.

### Gaps
- No quantitative data on recruiter or client reactions to disclosed AI in portfolios; the Cannes "40% of entries declared AI use" figure appeared in a search summary I could not trace to a specific page, so it is not used; The Rookies rules target student contests; Fueler, Uxfolio and Muzli are practitioner blogs; the Dafi, Cannes and EU items are search summaries only.
