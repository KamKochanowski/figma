## Q1. Visual language 2026 (type, colour, texture and tactility, 3D, glass, brutalism, editorial, bento, maximalism vs restraint, human craft)

### Takeaway
The 2026 baseline is a very large, regular-weight grotesk headline on a warm off-white (or a single saturated colour), and the reports and the data agree that differentiation now comes from an owned material vocabulary and visible human craft, not from a style. A key new finding: the gallery look (serif display with an italic accent word, mono labels, cream palette) overlaps substantially with what AI tools now generate by default (Anthropic's own frontend-design skill lists it as a tell), so it is peaking and risky unless chosen deliberately.

### Cited Findings

#### Typography (variable/kinetic, serif revival, mono, oversized, type as interface)
- Muzli (21 Oct 2026) loaded 2,190 sites featured by 24 galleries in 2026 (Awwwards, CSS Design Awards, SiteInspire, Land-book, One Page Love and others) and read the rendered fonts: 60% use one family for headline and body; Inter is on 13% (24% of Framer sites, 10% of Webflow sites, because it is the default); PP Neue Montreal 3.9%, Neue Haas Grotesk 3.0%, Suisse Int'l 2.1%, ABC Diatype 2.0%, Aeonik 1.3%, Helvetica Now 1.0%; Geist 3.5% and Geist Mono 4.1%; 7% rely on system fonts (Helvetica/Arial/Times included); 67% of headline fonts are paid or custom — [Muzli, Font pairings 2026](https://muz.li/blog/font-pairings-2026/)
- Same study, how it is set: median headline 64 px over 16 px body (4x); 42% of headlines at weight 400 and 16% at 500 (only 17% bold or heavier); 58% tracked tighter than default (mostly -1 to -3%); median line-height 1.0 — [Muzli, Font pairings 2026](https://muz.li/blog/font-pairings-2026/)

### Inferences
- The 2026 look that wins attention is not a style but an owned material vocabulary (Webflow's "proprietary effects", Anthropic's "materials and vernacular"). For a print/packaging AD the vocabulary already exists: sheets, cutting mats, registration marks, spot colours, overprint, riso, die lines. Oryzo's cutting mat and Grilli Type's technical sheet are close cousins, but I found no portfolio that uses it as the interface (consistent with the earlier 90-site finding).
- The gallery typography recipe (large regular-weight grotesk, optional serif with an italic accent word, mono labels) is simultaneously the most common designer look (Muzli) and an AI tell (Anthropic skill). The safe move is to choose type for a stated reason, use one family plus one distinctive face, and avoid italic-accent-word and eyebrow-caps habits.
- Glass should be optional or absent; if used, it must hold contrast across a user-controlled range.
- Off-white is the mainstream neutral (Cloud Dancer, nature distilled, Floema, Anthropic); a spot-colour system borrowed from print is a more ownable palette than cream plus terracotta.

## Q2. Interaction and motion (scroll, transitions, cursor, micro-interactions, sound, canvases, game-like/OS, personalisation)

### Takeaway
Motion is moving from "more effects" to "motion with a job": scroll as wayfinding, one orchestrated moment, user-triggered feedback, and progressive enhancement through native browser APIs. The generic tells are fade-up on every section and hover effects on every card; Figma Sites already ships custom cursors and marquees as presets, so pointer play no longer differentiates.

### Cited Findings
- Scroll: Webflow's trend 7 "guided scrolling" — last year's trend was scroll narratives, this year it is scroll as a navigation tool (progress indicators, numbered steps, a speedometer that tracks scroll velocity on Dropbox Dash's McLaren F1 page) to fight shrinking attention — [Webflow](https://webflow.com/blog/web-design-trends-2026); Figma #6 lists scrollytelling and micro-animations (Nike, Ralph Lauren) — [Figma](https://www.figma.com/resource-library/web-design-trends/)
- Anthropic's frontend-design skill: "fade-and-slide-up entrances on each section and hover transitions on every card are the generic default and read as AI-generated"; prefers "a single orchestrated moment" and motion that answers a person's action — [SKILL.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anthropics/skills/main/skills/frontend-design/SKILL.md); animation spam is a catalogued tell (1.1% of complaints in the playbook's data) — [claudecodehq](https://claudecodehq.com/playbooks/unslop-ui)
- Own audit: Lenis (smooth scroll) strings in 16 of 24 and GSAP strings in 15 of 24 award-winning sites, so the toolkit is the default of the genre — [table](#own-audit-table)
- Page transitions: same-document View Transitions were about to become Baseline Newly available with Firefox 144 (stable 14 Oct 2025); Chrome shipped cross-document transitions in 2024 and Safari added support; nested transition groups arrived in Chrome 140 — [Chrome for Developers, What's new in view transitions (2025 update)](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/view-transitions-in-2025) (last updated 8 Oct 2025)
- Interop 2026 (12 Feb 2026) keeps scroll-driven animations (`animation-timeline`, `view-timeline`) and view transitions (same-document and cross-document) as focus areas, along with anchor positioning, dialog/popover and `contrast-color()` — [web.dev, Interop 2026](https://web.dev/blog/interop-2026); conflicting secondary sources disagree on exact Firefox/Safari versions for scroll-driven animations, so verify on MDN/caniuse before shipping

## Q3. AI-native UX (conversational and generative interfaces, agents, adaptive content, agent-facing layers, well-designed vs gimmick)

### Takeaway
The credible 2026 pattern is two layers: a human-facing site that stays fast and scannable, with an optional conversational layer grounded in the owner's own content; and a machine-facing layer (Markdown for agents, llms.txt, NLWeb, WebMCP) that is cheap to add and is already used by some top independent developers. llms.txt has no proven SEO or AI-citation effect, so its value for Kamil is as a signal and a lead-qualification tool, not as traffic.

### Cited Findings

#### Conversational and generative interfaces on portfolios
- Figma: 2026 chatbots are "proactive, conversational, and often agentic"; 51% of Figma users working on AI products are building agents (vs 21% a year earlier, per Figma's 2025 AI report); also voice navigation and adaptive forms — [Figma](https://www.figma.com/resource-library/web-design-trends/); Figma's State of the Designer 2026 (906 designers): 72% use generative AI and 91% say it improves the quality of their outputs, not just speed — same page
- Bestfolios (11 Feb 2026) lists eight portfolios that use AI in earnest: a Shopify product designer's built-in chatbot, a student's chatbot plus AI avatar, an interactive playground of AI experiments, and AI case studies; its takeaways: "turn your portfolio into an interactive conversation, not just a static showcase" and "documenting iterations and decision-making makes your process visible and credible" — [Bestfolios](https://bestfolios.substack.com/p/how-designers-are-actually-using); a 2026 newsletter warns against making a chatbot the only way to review a portfolio — [Be Your Own Design Team](https://beyourowndesignteam.beehiiv.com/p/2025-portfolio-vs-2026-portfolio-what-changed) (search-result summary)
- Own check: Pearl Sequeira's homepage (cited by Bestfolios for a built-in chatbot) shows no visible chat UI in the first fold (`https://www.pearlsequeira.com/`), so the chatbot may live deeper in the site
- Live example of the pattern: Wassim Nasr's portfolio — a 3D character at a desk, a speech bubble, the prompt "Ask about my experience, projects, or skills..." and three prompt chips — `wassimnasr-talk-to-portfolio.jpg`, https://wassimnasr.com/ (CSS Design Awards nomination is from [RESEARCH_PORTFOLIO.md](/home/user/figma/docs/portfolio/RESEARCH_PORTFOLIO.md), not re-verified)
- Academic evidence on generative vs chat UI is thin: an ACL 2026 Findings paper reports generative interfaces outperform conversational ones across query types — [Generative Interfaces for Language Models](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.19227v2) (preprint abstract level; not independently checked)

#### Designed well vs gimmick
- NN/g, State of UX 2026: users and teams are fatigued by "lazy AI features and AI slop"; "the winners will treat AI as a tool that recedes into the background"; "trust will be a major design problem for AI experiences... transparency, control, consistency, and support when the system fails"; "UI is no longer a differentiator" because interactions are increasingly mediated by an AI layer, and "if you're just slapping together components from a design system, you're already replaceable by AI. What isn't easy to automate? Curated taste, research-informed contextual understanding, critical thinking, and careful judgment" — [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/state-of-ux-2026/)
- Cost and limits are real: Canva paused the roll-out of Canva AI 2.0 when 75M people used it, then cut cost per task by almost 90% — [Creative Bloq, 17 Sep 2026](https://www.creativebloq.com/design/design-software/why-canva-hit-the-brakes-on-its-huge-ai-rollout-and-what-other-brands-can-learn-from-it); Muzli reports Apple put a daily usage cap on Siri's AI features — [Muzli, Apple Event 2026](https://muz.li/blog/apple-event-2026-iphone-duo-designers/)

#### Layers for AI agents (llms.txt, Markdown, structured data, WebMCP, NLWeb)
- Muzli (Oct 2025) framed it as "From UX to MX: the machine experience": sites are read, interpreted and summarised by machines — [Muzli](https://muz.li/blog/web-design-trends-2026/)
- Google (guide last updated 10 Dec 2025): "You don't need to create new machine readable files, AI text files, or markup to appear in these features. There's also no special schema.org structured data that you need to add"; SEO fundamentals still apply — [Google Search Central, AI features and your website](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)
- llms.txt adoption: Originality.ai counted 36,120 llms.txt sites across 3M+ websites by May 2026 (8.8x in twelve months); Ahrefs (June 2026, 137,000 domains) found 97% of llms.txt files received zero requests in May 2026, AI retrieval bots made 1.1% of requests, GPTBot 4.51%, ClaudeBot 0.80%, and SEO audit tools were the top requesters — [PPC Land, 2 Jul 2026](https://ppc.land/llms-txt-adoption-rises-8-8x-but-97-of-files-get-zero-ai-requests/)
- John Mueller reportedly called llms.txt "purely speculative for now" (Reddit, June 2026) and pointed to WebMCP as the bigger agent-readiness priority; Chrome's Lighthouse audits llms.txt while Search says it is not needed — [Pasquale Pillitteri summary](https://pasqualepillitteri.it/en/news/3734/llms-txt-google-seo-chrome-lighthouse-en) (secondary SEO blog)
- WebMCP: a proposed web standard (JavaScript and annotated HTML forms) that lets a page declare tools for in-browser agents, with discovery, JSON-schema inputs and shared state; tools execute visibly on the page, sensitive actions can ask for confirmation, and it "can be added as a progressive enhancement"; docs published 18 May 2026, updated 7 Oct 2026, origin trial running — [Chrome for Developers, WebMCP](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/ai/webmcp); early preview was in Chrome 146 in Feb 2026 — [bug0 guide](https://bug0.com/blog/webmcp-chrome-146-guide) (secondary); sources conflict on whether `navigator.modelContext` was later replaced by `document.modelContext`, unverified
- NLWeb (Microsoft, 19 May 2025): an open project from R.V. Guha that builds natural-language interfaces from Schema.org/RSS data; "every NLWeb instance is also a Model Context Protocol server" — [Microsoft, Introducing NLWeb](https://news.microsoft.com/source/features/company-news/introducing-nlweb-bringing-conversational-interfaces-directly-to-the-web/); Cloudflare AI Search deploys an NLWeb Worker with `/ask` (for people) and `/mcp` (for agents) endpoints, public preview, docs updated 10 Jul 2026 — [Cloudflare docs](https://developers.cloudflare.com/ai-search/how-to/nlweb/) (announced 28 Aug 2025 per [Cloudflare blog](https://blog.cloudflare.com/conversational-search-with-nlweb-and-autorag))
- Cloudflare "Markdown for Agents" (Feb 2026; docs updated 9 Oct 2026): when a client sends `Accept: text/markdown`, Cloudflare converts the HTML to Markdown at the edge; available at no cost on Pro, Business and Enterprise plans (and SSL for SaaS), not listed for Free; 6 MiB HTML limit; Content-Signal header support — [Cloudflare docs](https://developers.cloudflare.com/fundamentals/reference/markdown-for-agents/); [changelog 12 Feb 2026](https://developers.cloudflare.com/changelog/post/2026-02-12-markdown-for-agents/)
- Own audit of live sites: 4 of 24 award-winning portfolio/studio sites serve `/llms.txt` (Lee Holmes, Davide Cattaneo, Jesper Landberg, Lorenzo Lottici), versus about 1.2% of the web (36,120 of 3M+); across the 44-site set 11 do (also Floema, New Studio, pen.dev, Rootly, Ryan Haskins and two Shopify stores) — [table](#own-audit-table)
- Best live example: Jesper Landberg (Awwwards Independent of the Year 2022 and 2024) says in `llms.txt` that "the site itself is a WebGL piece, so its content is served as Markdown for anything that is not a browser"; every URL answers `Accept: text/markdown` (I confirmed a `text/markdown` response) and `.md` paths; the file includes "When to use this" and "Not the right referral for" lists, so agents can qualify him — https://jesperlandberg.com/llms.txt ; screenshot `jesperlandberg-spatial-cards-md-for-agents.jpg`; Lorenzo Lottici, Lee Holmes, Floema and New Studio returned HTML for `Accept: text/markdown` in my test (Davide Cattaneo not tested)
- E-commerce platforms already ship agent layers: Liquid Death's and Marlene Print House's `/llms.txt` read "Agent Instructions" for shopping on a user's behalf "directly or through browser-based WebMCP tools" (Shop skill, UCP), and Liquid Death's page code registers WebMCP tools (`modelContext.registerTool`, which I confirmed in its HTML); pen.dev, Vacation and Marlene Print House match the same code pattern (regex hit, not manually verified); none of the 24 award-winning portfolios did — https://liquiddeath.com/llms.txt ; own audit, [table](#own-audit-table); screenshots `liquiddeath-packaging-maximalism.jpg`, `marlene-riso-print-shop.jpg`
- Structured data on award sites: 8 of 24 ship JSON-LD (mostly Person/WebSite/ProfessionalService) — own audit, [table](#own-audit-table); only 6 of 44 name AI crawlers in `robots.txt`

### Inferences
- A 2026 "AI specialist" signal that is not a chatbot: Markdown for agents plus a hand-written `llms.txt` that states what he is hired for and what he is not (the Landberg pattern), possibly an `/ask` endpoint and a couple of WebMCP tools (list case studies, fetch a case study, request a brief with user confirmation). On Cloudflare's Free plan the edge conversion may not be available, so Astro-generated `.md` routes are the portable option.
- Do not sell llms.txt as SEO; Google's guide and the Ahrefs log data argue against it. Sell it as a demonstration of understanding how agents will find and shortlist creatives.
- If a conversational layer is added: ground it in his real case studies, show sources/cards, keep a normal fast path, cap cost, and fail gracefully (NN/g trust criteria).

### Gaps
- No evidence found on how art directors or recruiters react to AI chat in portfolios; the Bestfolios list is curation, not outcomes.
- WebMCP is in flux (origin trial, naming conflicts); llms.txt evidence comes from SEO vendors; NLWeb adoption data not found.
- Awwwards' "AI in Design Report 2026" (a Site of the Day per search listing, [link](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/ai-in-design-report-2026)) and its AI conference talks could not be read.

## Q5. Accessibility and performance as trends (reduced motion, light/dark, low-carbon web)

### Takeaway
Accessibility and weight are becoming visible quality signals because the web at large is poor at both (median mobile home page 2.6 MB; contrast passes on 30% of sites; `prefers-contrast` on about 1%), while the lightest award-winning portfolios (two of them built on Astro, under 600 KB) show that craft and lightness co-exist. Apple's user-adjustable Liquid Glass shows the direction: users control motion, contrast and transparency.

### Cited Findings
- HTTP Archive Web Almanac 2025 (July 2025 data): median home page 2.9 MB desktop and 2.6 MB mobile (up 7-8% year on year); median mobile home page carries 632 KB JavaScript, 911 KB images, 122 KB fonts; 90th percentile desktop page 9.2 MB; home pages are about 46% heavier than inner pages — [Web Almanac, Page weight](https://almanac.httparchive.org/en/2025/page-weight)
- Core Web Vitals: 48% of mobile and 56% of desktop origins pass all three (2025) — [Web Almanac, Performance](https://almanac.httparchive.org/en/2025/performance) (figures via a secondary summary of the chapter)
- Accessibility Web Almanac 2025: median Lighthouse accessibility score about 85; only 30% of sites (31% on mobile) have sufficient text contrast; `prefers-reduced-motion` appears on about 50% of pages, `prefers-color-scheme` on about 13%, `forced-colors` on 16-19%, `prefers-contrast` on about 1%; automated tools catch under half of issues — [Web Almanac, Accessibility](https://almanac.httparchive.org/en/2025/accessibility)
- Figma's 2026 list includes sustainable web design (leaner code, optimised images, low-impact hosting) and states high-contrast palettes, screen-reader support, voice navigation and keyboard-only flows "are becoming standard"; dark mode "has become standard" with a toggle — [Figma](https://www.figma.com/resource-library/web-design-trends/)
- Apple's slider (iOS 27) plus NN/g's critique make user-controlled transparency and contrast a design requirement; Muzli's phrasing: "you're designing a range" — [MacRumors](https://macrumors.com/2026/06/10/how-liquid-glass-is-changing-in-ios-27); [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/liquid-glass/); [Muzli](https://muz.li/blog/apple-event-2026-iphone-duo-designers/)
- Interop 2026 includes `contrast-color()`, anchor positioning, dialog/popover — [web.dev](https://web.dev/blog/interop-2026)
- Low-carbon: the W3C Web Sustainability Guidelines are still a Group Note Draft (latest 2026 draft dated 29 Jul 2026) — [W3C WSG draft](https://www.w3.org/TR/2026/DNOTE-web-sustainability-guidelines-20260729/); the commonly quoted figure is about 0.36 g CO2e per page view (a data-centre plus network baseline), while methods that include devices give about 0.7-1.5 g — [Projet Celsius](https://projetcelsius.com/en/blog/website-carbon-footprint/); [University of Edinburgh](https://www.ed.ac.uk/news/cop-websites-bigger-emitters-than-average-pages)

## Q6. Fit for Kamil (print, packaging and motion art director positioning as "senior plus AI specialist"): ranked, example-backed trend map

### Takeaway
The strongest fit is an owned print-production vocabulary (sheet, mat, spot colour, overprint) delivered with editorial typography and one signature motion or 3D moment, a fast work-first front door, visible human process next to AI use, and a quiet agent-facing layer (Markdown, llms.txt as a referral guide, optional `/ask`). #### Best fits for a print, packaging and motion AD who wants to read as "senior plus AI specialist"
- Make the print world the UI: a work index laid out as a press sheet, hover or scroll revealing registration marks, spot-colour swatches, overprint and varnish toggles, with the packaging or key visual shown as a real 3D object (Blender to glTF) on a cutting-mat-style grid. This is tactile (Adobe, Canva, Wix), non-generic (Anthropic's "materials and vernacular") and uncontested (no portfolio found).
- Typography with print-grade detail (balanced headlines, hanging punctuation, optical sizing, tight tracking) using a foundry face rather than the Inter default; one family plus one voice.
- Work first: project list and a 20-30 s reel above the fold (as proposed in the earlier research), with the exploratory layer optional.
- Show human decisions next to AI use (sketch, prompt, selection, retouch, final), with plain disclosure; this is the "how it was made" authenticity the reports reward and the "curated taste" NN/g says is not automatable.
- The AI-specialist credential through infrastructure rather than a chatbot: a Markdown-for-agents version of every page, an `llms.txt` that says what he is hired for and what not (Landberg pattern), optionally an `/ask` endpoint (NLWeb on Cloudflare or a Claude-backed Worker) grounded in his case studies, and one or two WebMCP tools with confirmation. Present it honestly as an experiment, not as SEO.
- Accessibility and weight as senior signals: honour reduced motion, offer theme and motion toggles, keep the first view under about 1-2 MB, document it in a short "how this site is built" note.

#### What would make him look generic
- Anything on the AI-default lists: purple gradient and Inter; cream background, serif display, terracotta accent (note it is Anthropic's own palette); near-black with neon accent; eyebrow caps and mono labels on everything; italic accent word in headlines; numbered 01/02/03 markers on non-sequences.
- Reflexive glass, bento, fade-up, hover-scale, scrolljacking; a WebGL particle hero with no work behind it.
- A chatbot as the only way in, an "AI-powered" badge without receipts, filler copy and invented numbers.
- Template shells (OS, infinite canvas, sticker board) used without a personal system.
- Stock-looking AI imagery in case studies without process evidence.
